TASK: Sample-level Audio Core Cue Annotation

Use only audible acoustic/vocal evidence from the supplied audio payload.
The input manifest explicitly identifies whether the payload is one
complete audio file or a set of timestamped overlapping chunks. Do not
guess the input type from the number of audio payloads.

If input_type is "complete_audio", judge the supplied complete audio file.
If input_type is "overlapping_chunks", treat all supplied chunks and their
timestamps as views into the same continuous original timeline. If the
manifest says the chunks cover the full duration, judge whether the Cue
occurs anywhere across that covered duration. A clear Cue in any chunk can
support a sample-level positive label. If the manifest is missing,
inconsistent, or indicates incomplete coverage, do not assume that the
unobserved duration was checked.

Do not use subtitles, transcripts, video frames, emotion labels, or
information from another modality. Do not infer acoustic cues from what
the speaker says. Chunk boundaries and overlapping regions are
implementation artifacts: do not count a chunk boundary as a pause and
do not count the same event twice. Leading/trailing file silence, missing
audio, padding, or decoder failure is not automatically a speaker pause.

Cue definitions:

1. AUD_SPEECH_RATE_REL_FAST
At least one speech segment is clearly faster than a reliable,
comparable within-sample/same-speaker baseline.

2. AUD_SPEECH_RATE_REL_SLOW
At least one speech segment is clearly slower than a reliable,
comparable within-sample/same-speaker baseline.

3. AUD_PITCH_LEVEL_REL_HIGH
At least one comparable voiced segment has clearly higher F0 than the
within-sample/same-speaker baseline.

4. AUD_PITCH_LEVEL_REL_LOW
At least one comparable voiced segment has clearly lower F0 than the
within-sample/same-speaker baseline.

5. AUD_VOCAL_INTENSITY_REL_HIGH
At least one comparable speech segment has clearly higher vocal intensity
than the within-sample baseline when recording conditions allow comparison.

6. AUD_VOCAL_INTENSITY_REL_LOW
At least one comparable speech segment has clearly lower vocal intensity
than the within-sample baseline when recording conditions allow comparison.

For cues 1–6:
- Never compare absolute pitch, rate, or loudness across different speakers.
- Do not confuse microphone distance, gain changes, background noise,
  clipping, compression, or recording-gain changes with a vocal change.
- A comparable speech unit normally means a distinct phrase/utterance or
  another segment long enough to estimate the relevant feature; two
  arbitrary adjacent words are not automatically two comparable units.
- Follow this decision flow for each relative Cue:
  1. Are there at least two comparable speech segments with adequate
     signal quality for this feature? If NO, use unobservable.
  2. Is there a clear within-sample difference in rate, F0, or vocal
     intensity that is not adequately explained by phonetic content,
     sentence type, linguistic emphasis, or recording artifacts?
     If YES and pronounced enough to meet the frozen criterion, use
     positive for the corresponding relative Cue.
  3. If the comparison is valid and no segment meets the frozen criterion,
     use negative. If the difference is subtle or ambiguous, use uncertain.
     If recording artifacts make comparison unreliable, use unobservable.
- For F0 and intensity, rely on enough valid voiced/audio evidence to make
  the comparison; do not invent a frame count or numeric threshold in the
  prompt. Feature-quality thresholds belong to the frozen preprocessing
  configuration, not to model guesswork.
- Do not invent numeric thresholds; follow the frozen acoustic protocol.

7. AUD_PITCH_VARIABILITY_HIGH

8. AUD_PITCH_VARIABILITY_LOW
Clearly limited/narrow pitch variation across sufficiently observable speech.
Do not infer it from low loudness alone.

9. AUD_PAUSE_LONG
At least one internal silence interval between valid speech segments
reaches or exceeds the frozen long-pause duration threshold.

10. AUD_PAUSE_FREQUENT
Valid speech is repeatedly interrupted by internal pauses that meet the
frozen duration and frequency criteria.

CRITICAL: Count pauses across the complete covered timeline, not
independently per chunk. A pause appearing in an overlap region counts

11. AUD_INTONATION_RISING
Clearly rising acoustic intonation over a phrase/utterance. A question
mark or interrogative syntax alone is not evidence.

12. AUD_INTONATION_FALLING
Clearly falling acoustic intonation over a phrase/utterance.

13. AUD_INTONATION_FLAT
Clearly flat/monotonous intonation across enough observable speech.
One short flat fragment is insufficient to label the entire sample flat
unless the frozen sample-level rule is met.

14. AUD_VOICE_TENSE
Clearly audible vocal tension, strain, or pressed phonation. Loudness,
anger-related wording, or high pitch alone is insufficient.

15. AUD_VOICE_TREMBLING
Clearly audible voice instability/trembling. Ordinary pitch variation,
recording noise, or compression artifacts alone are insufficient.

16. AUD_LAUGHTER_PRESENT
Clearly audible laughter or chuckling at any point in the observed sample.
Do not infer laughter from humorous words.

17. AUD_CRYING_SOBBING_PRESENT
Clearly audible crying, sobbing, or a distinct crying vocal event.
Do not infer crying from sad words or low volume.
